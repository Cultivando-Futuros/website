About the program

Real science, close to home

We are not accepting applications yet. Check back soon: we will post how to apply when the program opens.

1. Why we started this

Young people in our farmworker communities are curious and smart. But many do not get the chance to do real science outside of class. We want to change that.

We also believe that people who live with a problem should help study it. Who knows your community better than you?

2. Four research teams

Each team has 3 or 4 high school students, 2 college students from UC Santa Cruz, a graduate research consultant from UC Berkeley, and several adult mentors, like scientists, health workers, and community leaders.

Epidemiology

Study health patterns in our community. Who gets sick, and why?

Environmental sciences

Study water, air, soil, or pesticides.

Medical science

Learn about health care and the health problems farmworker families face. You may get to shadow health care workers at Clínica de Salud del Valle de Salinas.

Science education

Make science learning tools for schools and the community, or study how science is taught.

3. Everyone learns. Everyone teaches.

We call this "mentoring the mentor."

  • Scientists and community leaders guide the whole team.
  • Graduate students from UC Berkeley give research advice to the teams.
  • College students from UC Santa Cruz work side by side with you.
  • You lead your team's project and help each other grow.

4. Your year, step by step

  1. SpringMeet the whole group. Every two weeks, hear from guest speakers like farmers, health workers, community advocates, and scientists. They also share how they got their jobs.
  2. Late springJoin a team and pick a question to study.
  3. SummerDo your research. Learn new skills in hands-on workshops. (Workshops continue into the fall.)
  4. Fall and winterLook at your results and figure out what they mean.
  5. End of the programShare what you found with the community and local leaders.

5. What you will make and share

  • Your team writes a short, two-page plan for its project. The program pays for supplies, like testing kits.
  • In hands-on workshops, you learn research ethics, how to write a survey, basic statistics, and how scientists measure pollution.
  • Before presenting to the public, each team practices in front of all the mentors and gets helpful feedback.
  • Teams share their findings with families, schools, local leaders, and the county agricultural commissioner.
  • Your team makes a real product, like a poster, a short paper, a podcast, or a teaching tool. Your name goes on it as an author.
  • Everyone keeps an online journal (ePortfolio) about their work and ideas.

6. Where it happens

Most activities take place at the CHAMACOS office on the Natividad campus in Salinas, at local schools, or at places in the community. To apply, you must speak and understand both English and Spanish. We use both languages in all activities.

7. Two groups of students

We will run the program two times.

  • Group 1: dates coming soon
  • Group 2: about a year after Group 1

If you can't join Group 1, you may be able to join Group 2.

8. Built on trust

This program grows out of more than 25 years of work with Salinas Valley families through the CHAMACOS study at UC Berkeley. CHAMACOS has produced more than 200 scientific papers and helped shape pesticide rules. In earlier CHAMACOS youth projects, high school students co-wrote scientific papers and helped paint a mural.

Cultivando Futuros is a partnership of UC Berkeley, UC Santa Cruz, CSU Monterey Bay, Clínica de Salud del Valle de Salinas, and the Center for Community Advocacy.
