For teachers, counselors and principals

Help us find curious students

For teachers, counselors, and principals in the Salinas and Pajaro Valleys. You know which students light up when they ask "why?" Those are the students we want to meet.

We are not accepting applications yet. Check back soon: we will post how to apply when the program opens.

1. The program at a glance

  • About 15 months of hands-on research with mentors from UC Santa Cruz and UC Berkeley
  • $2,000 stipend for each high school student
  • About 14 spots in each group
  • Free for students and families
  • There is no cost to your school.

2. Who to nominate

  • Students in 10th or 11th grade who go to high school in the Salinas or Pajaro Valley
  • Students who speak and understand both English and Spanish
  • Students who are curious about science, health, or the environment and care about their community
  • Especially students from farmworker families, and students who have had few chances to do science outside of class
  • Students who can come to meetings every two weeks and do research in the summer, with a parent or guardian's permission

Grades and test scores do not matter. We do not use them to choose students.

3. What students will do

Students practice real science, just like professionals do:

  • Ask a question about their community
  • Plan a study and collect data
  • Look at the results and figure out what they mean
  • Share what they learned with community members and local leaders

They work on one of four teams: epidemiology, environmental sciences, medical science, or science education.

4. How we choose students

The program's Steering Committee reads every application. We look at:

  • Interest in science and in their community's health and environment
  • How many science opportunities the student has had before (fewer is okay!)
  • Ability to commit, including in the summer
  • A good mix of schools, grades, and interests

If more students apply than we have spots, older students get the first spots, and younger students go on a waitlist.

5. Why it matters

Students do real research, present to local leaders, and build relationships with college students and scientists. These experiences can make college applications stronger and open doors to science and health careers. Many students work to help their families. The $2,000 stipend lets them take part without losing income. We will also share what students learn with school boards and counselors.

6. How you can help

  1. Think of students who might be a good fit.
  2. Share this website with them and their families.
  3. Help families with questions, or connect them with our Field Coordinator.
  4. Principals: allow us to post information or hold an information session for students and families.

Nominations and applications are not open yet. Check back soon. We will post a short nomination form and the application here when the program opens.

7. Safety and privacy

  • Our application never asks about immigration status or Social Security numbers.
  • Only the program's Steering Committee sees the applications.
  • We will add information about safety training and background checks for adults once confirmed.
  • Students apply only for the program. Everyone who joins is invited to also take part in a research study about the program, reviewed by UC Berkeley's research ethics board (IRB). The study is optional, and saying no never affects selection or the stipend.

8. Who is behind it

  • UC Berkeley School of Public Health (lead)
  • UC Santa Cruz
  • CSU Monterey Bay
  • Clínica de Salud del Valle de Salinas
  • Center for Community Advocacy

The program is funded by the Spencer Foundation.
